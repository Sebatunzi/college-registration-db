Presentation Notes: College Registration System Database Design

SHEMA SEBATUNZI Samuel  ·  BSCSR/2026/69594  ·  21 October 2026  ·  About 6–8 minutes

Slide 1: Title ~30 s

"Good evening. I am Shema Sebatunzi Samuel. I will present the database design for a college registration system, from the requirements to the final relational tables built in Draw.io."

Slide 2: Requirements: users & processes ~1 min

  • Goal: let students register for the courses in their programme each semester.
  • Five users: Student, Registrar, Lecturer, Head of Department, System Administrator.
  • Main flow: enrol student → student registers (Pending) → Registrar approves (Approved) → lecturer enters grade (Completed).
  • Out of scope: fees, timetabling, admissions.

Slide 3: Entities & business rules ~1.5 min

  • Entities: Student, Programme, Course, Registration and Lecturer, plus Department to group programmes and staff.
  • A student belongs to one programme; a course has one coordinating lecturer.
  • A programme has many courses, and a course can be shared by many programmes (many-to-many).
  • A student can register for a course only once per semester.
  • A grade can be entered only when the registration is Completed.
"Each rule is enforced by the database itself, through keys and constraints, not only by the application."

Slide 4: Conceptual model (ERD) ~1.5 min

  • Rectangles are entities, diamonds are relationships, and 1 / N / M show cardinality.
  • One-to-many: Department–Programme, Department–Lecturer, Programme–Student, Lecturer–Course.
  • Two many-to-many links: Programme–Course and Student–Course (through Registration).

Slide 5: Relational schema & data types ~1.5 min

  • 7 tables. Every table has a primary key (yellow); foreign keys are blue.
  • Each one-to-many relationship becomes a foreign key on the "many" side.
  • Each many-to-many relationship becomes a junction table: PROGRAMME_COURSE and REGISTRATION.
  • Data types: INT for keys, VARCHAR for names and codes, DATE for dates, SMALLINT for credits and semesters, BOOLEAN for flags.
  • The design is normalised to Third Normal Form, so nothing is stored twice.

Slide 6: Implementation & checks ~1 min

  • I implemented the design in SQL and tested it with sample data.
  • The database rejected every invalid entry: a duplicate registration, an unknown student, an invalid value, an early grade, and deleting a department that is still in use.
"In conclusion, the design meets the requirements: every table has a primary key, the relationships are consistent, and the business rules are enforced. Thank you. I welcome your questions."

Likely questions & short answers

Why add a Department entity?Programmes and lecturers both belong to a department. Storing it once avoids repeating department details.
Why is REGISTRATION a separate table?Student–Course is many-to-many. The registration table resolves it and stores the year, semester, status and grade.
What is PROGRAMME_COURSE?A junction table for the programme curriculum. Its primary key is the pair (programme_id, course_id).
How do you stop double registration?A UNIQUE constraint on (student, course, academic year, semester).
Why surrogate (INT) keys?They are short, stable and fast to join. Real-world codes such as reg_number stay UNIQUE.
Is it normalised?Yes, to 3NF. There are no repeating groups and no partial or transitive dependencies.
What happens if a department is deleted?It is blocked (ON DELETE RESTRICT) while programmes or lecturers still depend on it.
Which DBMS does it target?MySQL 8 or PostgreSQL. The SQL works on both.
